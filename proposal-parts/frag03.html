Section 3: Recommended Site Architecture

We designed this architecture for three priorities at once: clarity for visitors who arrive with intent, depth for AI retrieval across services and industries, and structure that survives a future brand refresh without a manual rebuild of every page.

Notes on the Architecture

Twenty-eight industry labels helped nobody choose CTW. They signaled breadth without signaling judgment. We consolidate into three to five named industries selected during the brand sprint with CTW leadership so each vertical page can carry depth, proof points, and retrieval-ready answers instead of thin taxonomy filler.

Transaction Services and Capital Markets sit as a peer pillar because the work is central to CTW's momentum, not an add-on folded under consulting. Visitors with M&A timing and deal questions should land in an obvious section with the same authority as Audit, Tax, and Consulting.

Most legacy Newsroom posts are several years old and several rule sets out of date. We retire the old newsroom archive for customer trust and retrieval quality, then relaunch Insights with a current cadence: articles, podcast, and original research living under one navigation label that matches how buyers actually browse.

Tools earns a top-level lane because calculators and assessments are conversion surfaces, not footer links. Treating them as first-class IA makes intent obvious and gives AEO clean, deep destinations to cite.

Final retain, rewrite, and retire calls on existing URLs are joint decisions during the audit. CTW keeps authority over what stays live. Our job is to map the recommendation set, not to pre-label every page dead before leadership signs off.
